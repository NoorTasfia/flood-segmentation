\begin{abstract}
Flood-segmentation models are usually judged by accuracy alone, yet responders run them on laptops, tablets and browsers, where latency, quantization behaviour and false alarms decide whether a model can be used. \emph{FloodLite} benchmarks three lightweight UNet students and a UNet$+$EfficientNet-B0 teacher on three folds of a five-fold cross-validation on the Flood Semantic Segmentation Dataset (FSSD), measuring FP32 and INT8 latency on an Apple M2~Pro CPU and in browser WebAssembly and ablating knowledge distillation (KD). On this saturated benchmark the ImageNet-pretrained students match or exceed the teacher without distillation (best IoU $\bestStudentIoU$ vs.\ $\teacherIoU$), and no KD configuration helps. Under one INT8 post-training quantization recipe, EfficientNet-Lite0 loses 0.043 IoU while MobileNetV3-Small and MobileViT-XXS collapse by 0.45--0.46. EfficientNet-Lite0 INT8 runs at \deployableMTwoFPS~FPS on the laptop CPU, $2.2\times$ faster and about $4\times$ smaller than the teacher. Zero-shot tests on overlap-verified external data expose a failure that FSSD metrics hide: because FSSD contains no water-free images, every FSSD-trained FP32 model predicts water on 70--100\% of dry scenes. Retraining on a combined corpus with 855 water-free images cuts student false-alarm rates to 3--9\%. On this less saturated corpus, distillation raises flood-only IoU by 0.03--0.07 for every student but also passes on the teacher's false alarms: distilled students fire on the teacher's false-alarm scenes more than twice as often as undistilled ones. For unattended flood monitoring, water-free training data is a requirement, and a teacher must be checked for false alarms before it is distilled from. Code, checkpoints and ONNX exports are released.
\end{abstract}
